🇪🇸 Retire abroad

Retire in Spain, priced from a Norwegian plan

Runway country notes 2026 figures Updated 25 July 2026

Spain is the classic sun move for Nordic early retirees, and its taxes are workable but layered: a savings-income scale rather than one flat rate, a wealth tax that depends on where you settle, and rental income on the ordinary scale.

What Runway models in Spain

  • Investment gains on the progressive savings scale, so the rate depends on how much you realise each year
  • The patrimonio wealth tax above its threshold, so a large pot pays a yearly levy like it would in Norway
  • Net rental income taxed on the ordinary progressive scale
  • Spending priced in euros, with your kroner converted through the FX scenario you choose

The move itself is part of the price

A move to Spain is never just Spain's tax rules. Leaving Norway with unrealised gains can trigger the exit tax, as if you sold on the way out, and Runway prices that into the plan before a single EUR of spending is modelled. The mechanics are in what the exit tax costs when you leave Norway, and the wider tooling question in which FIRE calculators handle a move abroad.

Once you are there, your spending runs in EUR through an exchange-rate scenario you control, so a weak krone decade is a plan you can stress, not a surprise.

Frequently asked

What tax does Runway assume on investment gains in Spain?+

Runway applies Spain's progressive savings scale to investment gains (the rate rises with the size of the gain), taxes net rent on the income scale, and models the patrimonio wealth tax above its thresholds. The figures are 2026, income-year-stamped, and refreshed as rules change.

Do I still pay Norway's exit tax if I retire to Spain?+

Yes. Leaving Norway with unrealised gains can trigger utflyttingsskatt, and Runway prices it into every plan that retires to Spain, as a cost of the move, before the destination's own rules take over on the drawdown.

Does Spain still have a wealth tax?+

Yes, and Runway models it. The state patrimonio applies above a threshold with big regional differences (some regions rebate it, and the solidarity levy backstops the top end), so treat the modelled figure as a representative estimate and check the region you would actually settle in.
